# Verity Protect Marketing Plan

## Product summary

Verity Protect is best positioned as a family-managed call safety product, not as a generic spam blocker.

Core flow:

- The family sets up call forwarding to a Verity number.
- Unknown callers are screened first and asked for a 6-digit Safety PIN.
- Trusted contacts bypass screening and connect directly.
- Calls that do not clear screening can be reviewed later with call details and transcripts.
- Family and circle members can see alerts, call history, and shared activity in one place.

## Best audience

Primary buyer:

- Adult children and caregivers, usually age 35-65, worried about a parent or grandparent getting hit by scams.

Primary beneficiary:

- Older adults who still want independence but need protection from suspicious calls.

Channel partners:

- Senior living facilities
- Home care agencies
- Geriatric care managers
- Elder law and senior service referral groups

## Best message

The strongest message is not "AI."

The strongest message is:

- Protect older adults without taking away independence.
- Let trusted callers through quickly.
- Force unknown callers to verify themselves.
- Give families clear visibility when something looks wrong.
- Keep the experience calm, simple, and readable.

## Messaging guardrails

Use:

- "Screens unknown callers before they reach your loved one."
- "Trusted contacts can get through directly."
- "Family members can review alerts and call history together."
- "Built for older adults, caregivers, and families."

Avoid or verify before using:

- Exact fraud-loss numbers without a source and date.
- "AI-powered" as the lead claim.
- "Stops all scam calls."
- "HIPAA compliant" unless you have formal support for that claim.
- Facility discount language unless the App Store offer path is ready.

## Biggest strategic note

Facility partnerships should be an endorsement and referral channel, not a dashboard product.

Recommended facility pitch:

- We give you a resident-safety resource.
- Families handle setup and management.
- Your staff does not monitor calls.
- Your staff does not become tech support.

## First 14 days

1. Turn warm installs into proof.
2. Tighten story and outreach copy.
3. Start local press and senior-safety outreach.
4. Start direct facility and agency outreach.
5. Delay paid ads until the landing page and proof assets are ready.

## First 14-day checklist

- Ask every current downloader for honest feedback by phone or text.
- Ask happy users for an App Store review.
- Capture 3 short testimonials.
- Record 1 short founder demo video.
- Create 1 printable one-pager for families.
- Create 1 printable facility handout.
- Build a tracking sheet for every outreach conversation.
- Reach out to 10 writers, 15 facilities, and 10 agencies in the first batch.

## 30-day channel plan

### 1. Warm network

Goal:

- Get reviews, testimonials, screenshots, and first referrals.

Actions:

- Personally follow up with everyone who already downloaded.
- Ask what confused them during setup.
- Ask whether they would recommend it to a friend with an aging parent.
- Ask for 1-sentence permissioned testimonials.

### 2. Press and local media

Goal:

- Earn credibility faster than ads can buy it.

Best angles:

- Young founder solving a real family problem
- Scam protection for older adults
- Local app launch with a clear public-interest angle

### 3. Facilities

Goal:

- Get brochures into welcome packets and family conversations.

Position:

- "Resident family safety resource"
- Not "software your staff has to manage"

### 4. Agencies and referral partners

Goal:

- Get trusted professionals to recommend Verity to families already worried about scams, confusion, or call safety.

### 5. Paid ads

Do not start with ads until:

- You have a clean landing page
- You have at least a few reviews or testimonials
- You know the main audience response angle

## Recommended use of the first $100

Use the first $100 only as a messaging test.

Recommended setup:

- One campaign
- Two creatives
- One audience problem per creative
- One landing page

Creative angle A:

- "Protect your parent from scam calls without taking away their independence."

Creative angle B:

- "Trusted callers get through. Unknown callers have to verify themselves."

Success metrics:

- Landing page click-through rate
- Cost per landing page visit
- App Store taps
- Demo or reply requests

Do not judge success only by installs on the first $100. The real job of this spend is to learn which message earns attention.

## Assets you should build next

- Adult-child landing page
- Facility one-pager
- Family one-pager
- Founder story paragraph
- FAQ for setup objections
- 30-60 second demo video
- Simple outreach tracker

## Metrics to track weekly

- App installs
- Paid conversions
- App Store review count
- Outreach emails sent
- Reply rate
- Calls or demos booked
- Facilities interested
- Agencies interested
- Testimonials collected

## Open decisions

- Do you want to lead with `older adults`, `seniors`, or `parents and grandparents`?
- Are you offering a trial, a price offer, or standard pricing only?
- Are you selling only to families right now, or also actively recruiting facility partners now?
- Are "agencies" senior-care agencies or marketing agencies?

## External source notes

If you use statistics in outreach, use dated official sources and include the year. Good current source candidates:

- FTC older-adult fraud reporting updates for 2024 and 2025
- FBI IC3 elder fraud materials for 2024
- Apple App Store Connect offer-code documentation if you want partner discounts
